# Memo: Where to Expand Digital Financial Access in Pakistan

**To:** Head of Strategy (hypothetical audience for this portfolio project)
**Re:** Financial inclusion gap and where branch-light areas need digital channels
**Data:** World Bank Global Findex (2011-2024); SBP scheduled bank branches by district (31 Dec 2020); PBS Population Census 2023 (district population)

## Summary

Pakistan has the lowest account ownership among the four countries compared, and the gap between men and women has widened. Bank branches are concentrated in Punjab, Sindh and Islamabad. Balochistan and Khyber Pakhtunkhwa have the lowest branch density, and about half of the districts in Balochistan fall below half the national average. Digital wallet and agent channels should be prioritised in these districts, with a women-focused product as a separate priority.

## Key findings

1. **Pakistan trails regional peers.** In 2024, 27.3% of adults 15+ had an account, against 89.0% in India, 81.7% in Sri Lanka and 43.3% in Bangladesh. Pakistan has improved since 2011 (10.3%), but the figure was flat between 2017 (21.3%) and 2021 (21.0%).

2. **The gender gap is large and growing.** In 2024, 42.3% of men had an account compared with 11.9% of women, a gap of 30.4 points. The gap was 14.3 points in 2011 and 14.7 points in 2021, so it has roughly doubled since 2021.

3. **Branch density is low outside the major cities.** Across the 119 districts with matched data, there are 5.95 branches per 100,000 people. Islamabad has 23.6. Balochistan (4.4) and KP (4.1) are well below Punjab (6.2) and Sindh (6.5).

4. **Low-density districts hold a large share of the population.** 39 of 119 districts (17.0% of the population) have less than half the national branch density. 50% of Balochistan's matched districts and 43% of KP's fall into this group, compared with 11% in Punjab and 0% in Islamabad.

## Recommendations

1. **Prioritise agent and wallet expansion in the lowest-density districts** (for example Musakhel, South Waziristan, Washuk, Orakzai, Torghar and Tharparkar, all under 1.1 branches per 100,000). Start with districts where population is large enough to justify agents, and check each one against household income data before committing (see limitations).

2. **Design a women-focused product as a separate initiative.** Examples include onboarding through female agents, family-linked accounts, and mobile-first access that doesn't require visiting a branch. Measure the gender gap in the target districts before and after launch.

3. **Use Balochistan and KP as a pilot for shared-infrastructure models**, such as agents working with existing telecom or microfinance partners, rather than building branches.

4. **Do not use branch counts alone to choose sites.** Combine this analysis with household income, mobile subscriber coverage and the share of adults already using wallets before any investment decision.

## Limitations

- **Branch counts are a supply measure.** They show where formal banks have a presence, not whether people use them. Mobile wallets and agents are not counted in this analysis.
- **Timing gap.** SBP branch counts are from December 2020, while population is from the 2023 census. Branches opened after 2020 are not counted, so the density figures overstate the gap in fast-growing districts.
- **Boundary changes.** Nine districts created after 2020 have no 2020 branch row and are excluded. Some other districts were redrawn, so their branch and population figures may not match exactly.
- **Excluded areas.** Azad Jammu & Kashmir and Gilgit-Baltistan are not in the PBS 2023 district table and are excluded (638 branches).
- **Low density is not always low demand.** Sparsely populated districts may have low demand for branches, so low density alone does not justify investment.
- **Findex and branch data measure different things.** Account ownership is survey-based. Branch counts come from a regulator's records. The two are compared for direction, not for exact matching.
